Why the ranking earns its place

Civic pride is a retention loop. People check back on a place they are attached to, and a rank gives them a reason to.

It makes trends legible. A score of 51 means little alone. "51, sixth of eight, down two places" is a story.

It creates pressure. Being last on a public list moves municipal budgets that a PDF report never will.

GUARDRAIL

Ranking without habitat matching is just geography. eDNAtlas normalises every score against a habitat-matched reference site, so 74 means the same thing in two different bays.

Under the hood

A FAIR pipeline, not a pretty front end.

Every stage emits something a machine can consume. The map is the last step, not the whole product.

01

Ingest

FASTQ, ASV tables, or a Darwin Core archive. Field metadata captured at submission, not reconstructed later.

02

Normalise

Mapped to Darwin Core plus the DNA-derived data extension. Units, CRS, markers, and primers reconciled.

03

Assign

Sequences matched against reference libraries. Confidence and the library version stored with the call.

04

Score

The Ecosystem Health Index computed from stored inputs. Recomputable, versioned, never hand-edited.

05

Serve

Open REST, bulk download, and the map. The same numbers through every door.

Ecosystem Health Index · components

SANTA MONICA BAY · 74

Three rules the score holds to

Recomputable. Stored inputs and versioned weights. Anyone can rerun the score and get ours.

Comparable. Normalised against a habitat-matched reference site.

Contestable. Every component links to the evidence behind it. Disagreement is a feature.

HONEST LIMIT

eDNA tells you what DNA was in the water, not how many individuals or whether they were alive. We label detections as detections and never print an abundance we did not measure.

SOURCES
Modeled on real Southern California sites and the FAIR eDNA methodology. Species, markers (12S MiFish, COI Leray), and reference libraries (MIDORI2, NCBI, CRUX) reflect taxa and tools documented for the region; site-level scores are illustrative until wired to live sequence data. Built to integrate the programs already doing this work: CALeDNA (University of California), SCCWRP Southern California Bight Regional Monitoring, the Marine Biodiversity Observation Network (MBON), CalCOFI, and OBIS / GBIF DNA-derived data.
